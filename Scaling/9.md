Good catch — is YAML mein **`scaleDown` section hi nahi hai**, sirf `scaleUp` define kiya gaya hai. Jab `scaleDown` missing hota hai, Kubernetes **default scaleDown behavior** apply karta hai automatically.

## Default scaleDown values (jab tum kuch specify nahi karte)

```yaml
scaleDown:
  stabilizationWindowSeconds: 300      # 5 minute wait
  policies:
  - type: Percent
    value: 100
    periodSeconds: 15
  selectPolicy: Max
```

## Iska matlab practically kya hai

### 1. `stabilizationWindowSeconds: 300`
HPA **last 5 minutes** ke recommendations mein se **maximum (sabse zyada conservative)** value chunega. Matlab agar kabhi bhi pichle 5 min mein spike aaya tha (zyada replicas chahiye the), to wo "yaad" rakhega aur scale-down nahi karega jab tak wo spike window se bahar na nikal jaye.

→ **Bahut cautious scale-down** — turant nahi ghatega, kam se kam 5 min ka buffer milega.

### 2. `type: Percent, value: 100, periodSeconds: 15`
Iska matlab — **current replicas ka 100%**, har **15 seconds** mein. 

Yani agar HPA decide kar le ki scale-down karna hai, to ye policy **practically koi limit nahi laga rahi** — ek hi step mein **replicas ko half ya zero tak** bhi le ja sakta hai! (100% of current = poora current count khud hata sakta hai single step mein.)

## Real example apne hi YAML pe

Maan lo current replicas = **40** (kyunki load spike tha), aur ab load gir gaya, sirf **8 replicas** ki zaroorat hai.

| Time | Kya ho raha hai |
|---|---|
| T=0:00 | Load gir gaya, raw recommendation = 8. Par stabilization window (300s) mein purana 40 ka data abhi bhi hai → HPA **kuch nahi karega**, replicas = **40** |
| T=5:00 | 5 minute beet gaye, purana spike data window se bahar nikal gaya. Ab stabilized rec = 8. Rate limit check: 100% of 40 = 40 (matlab kuch bhi allowed hai) → ek hi step mein replicas = **8** |

Dekho — **scale-down 5 minute tak bilkul ruka rehta hai**, lekin jab shuru hota hai to **bahut tezi se / ek hi jhatke mein** ho jata hai (kyunki 100% per 15s practically rate-limit nahi karta).

## Tumhare actual YAML mein iska overall effect

| Direction | Trust speed (stabilization) | Execution speed (periodSeconds/Percent/Pods) |
|---|---|---|
| **scaleUp** (jo tumne customize kiya) | Fast — sirf 30s | Fast — double ya +10 pods, jo zyada ho |
| **scaleDown** (default, kyunki specify nahi kiya) | Slow/cautious — 5 min wait | Practically **no limit** once it starts — ek step mein bahut bada drop ho sakta hai |

## Important real-world implication

Ye thoda **asymmetric/risky combination** hai:
- **Scale-up** tum chahte ho ki bahut fast ho (jo achi baat hai traffic spike handle karne ke liye).
- **Scale-down** 5 minute tak wait karega (good — safe), par jab karega to **bahut aggressively** ek hi step mein ho sakta hai (jaise 40 → 8 directly), jo kabhi-kabhi thoda risky ho sakta hai agar traffic phir se thoda badh jaye turant baad mein.

Agar tum chahte ho ki scale-down bhi **gradual** ho (jaise tumne pehle scaleUp ke liye kiya tha), to tumhe explicitly add karna padega:

```yaml
scaleDown:
  stabilizationWindowSeconds: 300
  policies:
  - type: Percent
    value: 10          # sirf 10% reduce karo
    periodSeconds: 60
  - type: Pods
    value: 5           # ya max 5 pods
    periodSeconds: 60
  selectPolicy: Min     # ← yahan Min use karo taki conservative wala chune!
```

**Note:** scaleDown mein agar tum gradual/safe behavior chahte ho, to `selectPolicy: Min` use karna better hota hai (jo dono policies mein se **chhota** reduction allow kare) — `Max` use karoge to wahi aggressive problem reh jayega.

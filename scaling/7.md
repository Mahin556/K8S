## The one-line version

- **`stabilizationWindowSeconds`** = "How much do I trust this new recommendation, or should I look at recent history before reacting?" → prevents **flapping** (scaling up and down repeatedly).
- **`periodSeconds`** (inside a `policies` entry) = "Once I've decided to scale, how fast am I allowed to do it?" → prevents **sudden, large jumps** in replica count.

One is about **whether/when to trust a decision**. The other is about **how fast to execute it.**

## A simple analogy: staffing a coffee shop

Imagine you decide barista headcount based on how long the line is.

- **`stabilizationWindowSeconds`**: Don't send someone home just because the line was short for 2 minutes — look at the busiest moment in the *last 30 minutes* and staff for that. This stops you from cutting staff and then immediately needing to call someone back in.
- **`periodSeconds`**: Even once you've decided you genuinely need 5 more baristas, you can only call in **1 new barista every 10 minutes** — because that's a sane onboarding pace. This limits how fast the change happens, regardless of how confident you are in the decision.

Notice these solve two completely different problems. You could trust a decision instantly (no stabilization) but still ramp it slowly (rate limit). Or you could be very cautious about trusting the decision but, once trusted, apply it all at once. They're independent knobs.

## How it actually works in HPA

```yaml
apiVersion: autoscaling/v2
kind: HorizontalPodAutoscaler
metadata:
  name: web-app-hpa
spec:
  scaleTargetRef:
    apiVersion: apps/v1
    kind: Deployment
    name: web-app
  minReplicas: 2
  maxReplicas: 12
  metrics:
  - type: Resource
    resource:
      name: cpu
      target:
        type: Utilization
        averageUtilization: 50
  behavior:
    scaleUp:
      stabilizationWindowSeconds: 30      # short window — react fast to spikes
      policies:
      - type: Pods
        value: 2
        periodSeconds: 60                 # but only +2 pods per 60s
      selectPolicy: Max
    scaleDown:
      stabilizationWindowSeconds: 300     # 5 min window — be very cautious
      policies:
      - type: Pods
        value: 1
        periodSeconds: 120                # only -1 pod per 120s
      selectPolicy: Min
```

Every ~15 seconds (the HPA controller's sync loop), it does roughly this for each direction:

1. Look at current metrics → compute a **raw recommended replica count**.
2. Apply **`stabilizationWindowSeconds`**: look back over that many seconds of *past* raw recommendations and pick the most conservative one — the **maximum** for scale-down (don't shrink too soon), the **minimum** for scale-up (don't grow on a blip). Call this the **stabilized recommendation**.
3. Apply the **`periodSeconds`** policy/policies: cap how much the replica count is actually allowed to move, based on a sliding rate limit (e.g., "no more than 2 pods added per 60s").
4. The final number is whatever survives both filters (clamped to `minReplicas`/`maxReplicas`).

## Detailed walkthrough: a traffic spike then a drop

Say `web-app` starts at **3 replicas**. Traffic spikes hard at T=0, pushing CPU way over target, then drops back down at T=10:00.

**Scale-up phase** (window=30s, rate limit = +2 pods/60s):

| Time | CPU metric | Raw recommendation | Stabilized rec. (min over last 30s) | Rate-limit allows | Actual replicas |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T=0:00 | 90% | 8 | 8 (only data point) | +2 (from 3) | **5** |
| T=0:15 | 92% | 8 | 8 | window still open from 0:00, 0 left | **5** |
| T=1:00 | 91% | 8 | 8 | +2 (window reset) | **7** |
| T=1:15 | 90% | 8 | 8 | 0 left in this window | **7** |
| T=2:00 | 88% | 8 | 8 | +1 (capped at target) | **8** |

Notice: the controller "knew" it wanted 8 replicas from the very first reading (stabilization window is short, so it trusted that quickly) — but `periodSeconds` forced it to **ramp up gradually** (5 → 7 → 8) instead of jumping straight to 8. That's the rate limit doing its job, independent of how fast the recommendation was trusted.

**Scale-down phase** (window=300s, rate limit = -1 pod/120s), starting at T=10:00 when load drops to needing only 3 replicas:

| Time | Raw recommendation | Stabilized rec. (max over last 300s) | Rate-limit allows | Actual replicas |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T=10:00 | 3 | 8 *(the spike is still inside the 5-min window!)* | n/a — no change | **8** |
| T=13:00 | 3 | 3 *(spike has finally aged out of the window)* | -1 | **7** |
| T=15:00 | 3 | 3 | -1 (new 120s window) | **6** |
| T=17:00 | 3 | 3 | -1 | **5** |
| T=19:00 | 3 | 3 | -1 | **4** |
| T=21:00 | 3 | 3 | -1 | **3** |

Here you can see both mechanisms stacking up to make scale-down deliberately slow:
- For the first ~3 minutes, **nothing happens at all** — not because of a rate limit, but because `stabilizationWindowSeconds=300` makes the controller keep "remembering" that old spike of 8 until it falls out of the 5-minute window.
- Once it's finally willing to act, `periodSeconds` then drags it out further, removing only 1 pod every 2 minutes.

## Why this distinction matters in practice

A very common gotcha: people set a long `stabilizationWindowSeconds` for scale-down expecting it to also smooth out the *rate* of scale-down, then are confused when the deployment still drops several replicas in one shot. That's because the **default scale-down policy is "100% per 15s"** — i.e., effectively no rate limit at all. The stabilization window only controls *when* it starts scaling down, not *how fast* it goes once it starts. If you want a slow, gradual scale-down, you need to set `periodSeconds`/`value` on the policy explicitly — stabilization alone won't give you that.

## TL;DR comparison

| | `stabilizationWindowSeconds` | `periodSeconds` (in a policy) |
|---|---|---|
| **Question it answers** | "Should I trust this new number yet?" | "How fast can I act on it?" |
| **Mechanism** | Looks backward over N seconds of *recommendations*, picks the most conservative one | Rate-limits the *actual replica change* over a sliding N-second window |
| **Purpose** | Stops flapping / reacting to noisy blips | Stops sudden large jumps in pod count |
| **Default scale-up** | 0s (react instantly) | double pods or +4, whichever bigger, per 15s |
| **Default scale-down** | 300s (wait 5 min) | 100% per 15s (no real cap once triggered) |

### What execly is CPU throttling?

CPU throttling is the intentional slowdown of a processor's performance by reducing its clock speed. It acts as an automated safety mechanism used by devices like laptops, phones, and desktops to prevent overheating, conserve battery life, or comply with power limits. [1, 2, 3, 4]  
Why Does It Happen? 

• Thermal Throttling (Heat): If you are pushing your system, the CPU generates immense heat. If temperatures reach a dangerous threshold (usually around 90-100°C), the processor slows down to cool off and avoid permanent hardware damage. 
• Power & Battery Conservation: To save power or extend battery life, devices will automatically lower CPU speeds. This also occurs if you are using an underpowered or faulty charger. 
• Software & System Limits: In multi-tenant systems or software containers (like Kubernetes), throttling restricts processes from using more than their fair share of resources. [1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9]  

The Impact on You 
When your CPU throttles, you will experience noticeable performance drops, lag, or stuttering. For example, a heavy video render may take much longer, or your games may drop frames while you play. [6, 8, 10, 11, 12]  
How to Fix It 
If you frequently experience sluggish performance, you can usually resolve the issue by addressing the root cause: 

• Improve Airflow: Ensure your device's vents aren't blocked, clean out dust, and avoid placing laptops on soft surfaces like beds or rugs. 
• Monitor Temperatures: You can track your hardware's temperature in real-time using tools like  MSI Afterburner 
 or  HWiNFO 
 to see if heat is the culprit. 
• Adjust Power Settings: Ensure your operating system (like Windows) is set to "High Performance" rather than "Power Saver" if you are plugged into the wall. 
• Check Hardware: In older devices, the thermal paste that helps transfer heat away from the processor may have dried out and require replacement. [4, 8, 14, 15, 16]
